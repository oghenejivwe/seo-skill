# Local business branch

Read this only if the project has a physical location or serves a defined geographic area. If it's a product, SaaS, directory or anything national, skip it — the main track applies and this one doesn't.

Local is a different game, not a variant of the same one. **You're ranking a Google Business Profile in a map, not a URL in a list.** Roughly two-thirds of clicks go to the top three map results, and position four is effectively invisible.

---

## The keyword framework in `02-keywords.md` barely applies

Search volume data is unreliable at local level and the third-party tools don't carry local-modifier data at all. Instead:

**Your keyword list is your Google Business Profile.** The primary category is your primary keyword. Secondary categories and listed services are your secondary keywords. That's the spine.

Use Search Console and a geo-filtered Keyword Planner to catch variants you'd otherwise miss, but don't let a zero-volume reading kill a service you actually offer.

---

## Diagnose before building anything

The equivalent of a keyword plan here is a **rank grid**: your profile's ranking position sampled across a grid of points covering the service area. Green for positions one to three, which are visible. Everything else isn't.

The metric that drives every subsequent decision is **top-3%** — what fraction of the grid is green.

```
1. Run a grid for your primary keyword
2. Run grids for the top 3–4 competitors
3. The best competitor's top-3% is your realistic ceiling
4. Your threshold = 25–50% of that ceiling
```

Then:

- **Below threshold** — Google doesn't believe you're an expert at this service. Build topical depth: answer the specific questions people ask about it, each one linked from the relevant service page.
- **At or above** — Google trusts you; you're just geographically limited. Build geographic depth: find grid cells sitting at positions four to six and write genuinely local pages about those areas.

Getting this backwards is the most common expensive mistake in local SEO. Location pages built before topical authority exists do nothing at all.

## Google Business Profile

The primary asset. Most competitors fill in a fraction of it, which is why it's winnable.

```
[ ] Claimed and verified
[ ] Categories: 2–4 minimum, up to 10 allowed
      Choose from Google's official list. Models invent categories
      that don't exist — it's a fixed dropdown, so look it up.
[ ] Services: 20–40, grouped under the category each belongs to
      This is a free text field. Be thorough.
[ ] Description: use all 750 characters
[ ] Every attribute answered, yes or no
[ ] Service areas: every suburb served, not just the main city
[ ] Hours, phone, website, booking link, opening date
[ ] Photos: 20 to start, 100+ as the target
[ ] Posts weekly
[ ] Reviews: a repeatable way to ask, and a reply to every one
```

On reviews, what's measured is not just the count and average: recency, how detailed the text is, whether photos are attached, and how established the reviewer is all feed in. A QR code that opens the review form directly beats asking people to look you up, and asking face to face after the work is done beats every automated prompt.

## The website's job

The site exists to make the profile rank. Two parts.

**The page the profile links to** must look like the same business. Title and H1 containing the category and the city, a map embed, reviews displayed, and the name, address and phone matching the profile character for character. Add `LocalBusiness` schema.

**The rest of the site mirrors the profile's structure.** One page per category, one page per service, linked from body copy in that same hierarchy. If the profile says you do something, there should be a page for it.

---

## Citations and local links

Citations are directory listings. Their only job is consistency: the same name, address and phone everywhere.

Local links come from relationships and money rather than merit, because nobody links editorially to a local service business:

- Chamber of commerce — the strongest local signal, and you can join several
- Sponsorships: youth sport, charities, community events, school associations. Check they list sponsors with links before paying
- Industry-specific directories
- Partnership pages with businesses you actually work with

---

## Local content

**Below threshold:** find the questions people ask about your services, reword them, answer each one briefly on the relevant service page, and link from that answer to a page answering it properly.

**At or above:** pick landmarks that exist on Google Maps — neighbourhoods, parks, major intersections, schools — near the grid cells where you rank four to six. Write about what's genuinely specific to that area: the age of the housing stock, access constraints, local regulations, what actually goes wrong there.

**Never produce location pages by find-and-replace.** Swapping a city name and padding with landmark trivia is a pattern search engines specifically target. The page has to say something true about that place that it couldn't say about anywhere else.
